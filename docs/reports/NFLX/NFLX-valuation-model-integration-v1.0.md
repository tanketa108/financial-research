# NFLX — Valuation model integration v1.0

**Model received:** `NFLX_v1_valuation_model.xlsx`  
**Purpose:** incorporate the user's Netflix valuation model into the published dashboard and company state without changing the workbook.

## Executive read-through

The model frames Netflix as a high-quality standalone compounder after the failed WBD bid, but with valuation already pricing demanding assumptions.

- Spot price used: **~$87**
- Median of 8 valuation methods: **~$77**
- Implied downside vs spot: **~11%**
- Valuation range: **~$54–100**
- Probability-weighted blended value: **~$84**
- Stance in model: **HOLD / slight UNDERWEIGHT at spot**
- Better risk/reward zone: **~$70–75**
- Sell / reduce zone: **>$110**

## Core model message

Netflix enters 2026 with strong financial quality: FY25 revenue around $45.2bn, operating margin around 29.5%, FCF around $9.5bn and materially better cash conversion than the 2021–2022 content investment cycle.

The failed WBD acquisition removes balance-sheet and integration uncertainty, which is positive for the standalone thesis and keeps capital allocation focused on buybacks and organic growth.

However, the model argues that the market price already embeds optimistic assumptions: sustained growth above the base case, further margin expansion and/or a lower risk premium.

## Key underwriting debates

1. **Margin expansion:** terminal margin needs to move toward the high-30s for the current spot to be more attractive.
2. **Revenue durability:** base revenue CAGR is below what the market appears to price.
3. **Ads and live events:** upside depends on ad-tier scaling, premium inventory and monetization of live/sports/event content.
4. **Content ROI:** Netflix's economic capex is content spend, already reflected in OCF/FCF rather than PP&E capex.
5. **Capital allocation:** post-WBD, watch buybacks versus any renewed large M&A appetite.
