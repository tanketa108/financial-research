## 2. Business overview

LVMH is the largest global luxury group, combining ~75 Maisons across wines & spirits, fashion/leather goods, perfumes/cosmetics, watches/jewelry, selective retailing and other activities. LVMH reports FY2025 revenue of **€80.807bn**, profit from recurring operations of **€17.755bn**, and Group-share net profit of **€10.878bn**. It operated **6,283 stores** at 2025 year-end.

Economic engine:

- **Brand desirability + scarcity + craftsmanship** support pricing power and high gross margins.
- **Fashion & Leather Goods** is the economic core: 46.7% of FY2025 revenue and 74.4% of group recurring operating profit before other activities/eliminations.
- **Selective Retailing / Sephora** provides growth and retail scale but lower margins than core luxury brands.
- **Wines & Spirits** and **Watches & Jewelry** are more cyclical / inventory-heavy and sensitive to China/US demand, tourist flows and category cycles.
- **Vertically controlled distribution**: Fashion & Leather Goods is 95% retail revenue in 2025, supporting pricing, experience control and working-capital visibility.

Source: LVMH Financial Documents Dec. 31 2025, pp. 2-5 and segment sections.

## 5. Working capital and capex notes

FY2025 cash flow quality improved despite lower operating profit:

- Cash from operations before working capital fell to **€24.941bn** from **€27.220bn**, mainly due to lower operating profit.
- Change in working capital was a **€576m cash requirement**, materially better than the **€1.925bn** requirement in 2024.
- Working capital requirement in 2025 mainly reflected **inventory increase of €1.315bn**; trade payables and other receivables/payables generated resources of **€216m** and **€303m** respectively.
- LVMH says Fashion & Leather Goods, Wines & Spirits and Perfumes & Cosmetics were the main contributors to cash requirements.
- Operating investments fell to **€4.567bn** from **€5.531bn**, mainly retail network investments by Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior, Tiffany and Sephora, plus production equipment for Parfums Christian Dior, champagne houses and Hennessy, and hospitality.

Source: 2025 Financial Documents pp. 19-20.

## 10. Risks

Model-relevant risks:

1. **Fashion & Leather Goods concentration:** 46.7% of FY2025 revenue and ~74% of segment recurring operating profit before eliminations; valuation sensitivity is dominated by Louis Vuitton/Dior trajectory.
2. **Luxury cycle / aspirational demand:** revenue declined 5% reported and 1% organic in FY2025; Q1 2026 shows only +1% organic with -6% reported due to FX.
3. **China / Asia exposure:** Asia excl. Japan was 26% of FY2025 revenue and 32% of Q1 2026 revenue; recovery quality is central.
4. **FX:** Q1 2026 currency headwind was -7%; FY2025 revenue FX impact was -3%.
5. **Geopolitics and tariffs:** Middle East conflict affected Q1 2026; management flags US tariffs and China anti-dumping measures in Wines & Spirits.
6. **Inventory / working capital:** FY2025 working capital need was lower but inventories still drove €1.315bn cash requirement.
7. **Category-specific weakness:** Wines & Spirits remains pressured after exceptional years; US and China cognac are important.
8. **Brand heat / creative transitions:** Dior, Celine, Loewe, Givenchy, Fendi creative changes can create upside or execution risk.
9. **Selective Retailing / DFS restructuring:** asset disposals and tourism recovery affect margin trajectory and comparability.
10. **Valuation risk:** high-quality luxury assets can derate sharply if growth normalizes, FX turns adverse, or margins reset lower.

## 11. Valuation variables to feed model

Priority inputs:

- Revenue growth by segment: especially Fashion & Leather Goods, Watches & Jewelry, Sephora/Selective Retailing.
- Organic vs FX/structure bridge: model reported revenue separately from organic demand.
- Segment margin trajectory: F&LG from 39.9% in 2023 to 35.0% in 2025; group recurring operating margin from 26.5% to 22.0%.
- Capex intensity: 2021-2025 operating investments / revenue range 4.1%-8.7%; FY2025 5.7%.
- Lease-adjusted FCF: use LVMH operating FCF definition to avoid overstating cash generation.
- Working capital: normalize inventory cycle; FY2025 requirement €576m vs €1.925bn in 2024.
- Tax rate: FY2025 effective tax rate 32.8%, inflated by additional French tax; normalize carefully.
- Debt cost / liquidity: net financial debt low at €6.857bn; gross debt maturity wall in 2026 manageable against €13.5bn cash/current AFS and €10.8bn undrawn lines.
- Capital returns: dividend per share stable at €13.00 for 2023-2025; buybacks material in 2025 but exact recurring policy needs URD detail.
- Terminal quality: brand durability, pricing power, DTC control and reinvestment runway justify premium but must be stress-tested.
